# FFAMuonTarget

G4beamline setup for an **FFA internal-target pion/muon source**: a 1 GeV
proton beam is injected into a fixed-field alternating-gradient ring with a
thin graphite target in one straight and an RF cavity in another. Every
turn the protons lose ~2 MeV in the target and the cavity puts it back, so
each proton keeps striking the target **at full energy** until it undergoes
a nuclear interaction. The π⁻ produced (the wrong rigidity and charge to be
contained) stream out of the ring near the target, are captured into a
solenoid decay channel, and yield μ⁻ at its end.

This is the ERIT concept (energy-recovery internal target, demonstrated at
KURRI at 11 MeV for neutron production) applied to π⁻/μ⁻ production at
1 GeV. The FFA is the natural host ring because its huge transverse and
momentum acceptance swallows the multiple scattering and energy straggling
that the target inflicts on the beam every turn.

Everything runs inside the project docker image
`ghcr.io/lawrenceleejr/g4beamline:sha-7a8bfe1` (G4beamline 3.08,
Geant4 11.0.2, physics list QGSP_BERT) through the wrapper
`scripts/g4bl-docker.sh`. The analysis scripts need only python3 + numpy
on the host.

---

## Quick start

```bash
# 0. one-time: generate the FFA cell field map (needs numpy)
python3 scripts/make_ffa_fieldmap.py --k 3.6 -o out/ffa_cell_map.txt

# 1. recirculating-target ring (defaults: 200 protons, 5 mm graphite, RF on)
./scripts/g4bl-docker.sh ffa_ring.g4bl
python3 scripts/analyze_ring.py            # turns, energy balance, pi-/mu- yields

#    ... or in parallel (4 docker containers x 75 protons):
./scripts/run_ring_parallel.sh 4 75

# 2. pi- -> mu- decay channel, fed by the ring output
python3 scripts/make_channel_input.py runs/job*/out    # capture acceptance
./scripts/g4bl-docker.sh pion_decay_channel.g4bl
python3 scripts/analyze_channel.py --nprotons 300      # mu- per proton

# 3. standalone target study: pi- yield vs proton energy ("what energy is best?")
./scripts/run_energy_scan.sh 8000 20
```

## The ring

The lattice is generated by `scripts/make_ffa_fieldmap.py` as a field map
of a single cell (2 cm grid, Maxwell-consistent off-midplane expansion to
3rd order in y) which `ffa_ring.g4bl` places 12 times, rotated about the
vertical axis through the ring center (the global origin). The deck then
adds the target, the cavity, the enclosure detectors and the beam.

| parameter | value |
|---|---|
| type | radial-sector scaling FFA, B ∝ (r/r₀)^k |
| cells | 12 × DFD triplet (reverse-bending D), R₀ = 9.00 m |
| field index | k = 3.6  (γ_t = √(k+1) = 2.14, ring runs just below transition) |
| F sector | +48° bend, 14° azimuthal width, B(r₀) = +2.155 T |
| D sectors | −9° bend each, 3.5° wide, at azimuth ±(8°–11.5°), B(r₀) = −1.616 T |
| straights | ~1.1 m at every cell boundary (azimuth 15° + 30°·k) |
| injection | 1 GeV kinetic (p = 1.696 GeV/c, Bρ = 5.657 T·m) |
| closed orbit | r = 8920.9 mm at F centers, 8628.7 mm in the straights |
| revolution | T_rev = 212.35 ns (L = 55.71 m, β = 0.875) |
| tunes (tracked) | Qx = 2.34, Qy = 1.22 |
| momentum acceptance | ~±20 % at the chamber walls (8.25–9.65 m) |
| target | graphite, 5 mm thick (≈ 2.0 MeV/turn), azimuth 15° |
| RF | 405 MHz pillbox (h = 86), V_eff ≈ 2.3 MV, φ_s ≈ 30° on the rising side (stable below transition), azimuth 195° |

Measured behavior (from `scripts/find_closed_orbit.py` and
`scripts/tune_rf.py`): a bare proton circulates > 4500 turns with a
relative momentum drift of ~2×10⁻⁷/turn from integration; with target and
RF on, the mean beam energy stays locked at 1000 MeV for the ~50–80 turns
it takes a proton to interact (λ_inel ≈ 39 cm of graphite ≈ 78 passes of
5 mm; large-angle elastic scatters retire some protons to the walls
earlier, so the observed mean is ~50 turns).

Coordinates: ring center at the global origin, y vertical, beam circulates
counterclockwise (+x toward +z). An element at azimuth φ sits at
(r cos φ, 0, r sin φ) with `rotation=Y-φ`, which makes local z the beam
direction and local x radially outward.

## Results from this configuration

Numbers from the runs used to develop the setup (QGSP_BERT; modest
statistics by design — everything reruns in minutes on a laptop):

**Energy scan** (20 mm graphite, 8000 protons/point; `out/scan_summary.txt`):

| KE [MeV] | π⁻/p | π⁺/p | π⁻/π⁺ | π⁻ per interaction | π⁻/p/GeV |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 300 | 0 | 0 | – | 0 | 0 |
| 500 | 0 | 0.0033 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 800 | 0.0024 | 0.0111 | 0.19 | 0.044 | 0.0030 |
| 1000 | 0.0033 | 0.0138 | 0.22 | 0.061 | 0.0033 |
| 1500 | 0.0060 | 0.0213 | 0.27 | 0.107 | 0.0040 |
| 2000 | 0.0085 | 0.0256 | 0.32 | 0.147 | 0.0043 |
| 3000 | 0.0174 | 0.0303 | 0.55 | 0.310 | 0.0058 |
| 5000 | 0.0341 | 0.0491 | 0.66 | 0.621 | 0.0068 |

π⁻ production turns on near 0.8 GeV and keeps rising past 5 GeV; per unit
beam energy it is still rising but flattening above ~2–3 GeV. **A 1 GeV
beam is below the natural optimum — which is the gap the energy-recovery
ring closes** by making every proton interact at full energy.

**Ring** (5 mm target, RF on, 1 GeV, 1200 protons): mean 54 turns per
proton (median 36, tail to the 300-turn tracking limit); π⁻ + in-flight μ⁻
reaching the chamber: **0.027 ± 0.005 per injected proton** (≈ 8× the
single-pass thin-target yield, ≈ 45 % of the ideal "one full-energy
interaction per proton" limit of 0.061), mean momentum ≈ 220 MeV/c, all
within ±15° of the target azimuth.

For comparison, a 45 cm graphite beam-dump target at 1 GeV gives a similar
raw count (0.029 π⁻/p escaping the block) — but from a 45-cm-long,
self-shielding source. The ring concentrates the same production into a
5 mm spot in a known straight, which is what makes efficient capture
optics possible, and its π⁻ never have to climb out of a thick block
(no reabsorption on exit).

**Capture + decay channel** (5 T, 0.5 m bore, 30 m): the channel mouth
sits at the target straight, pointed outward along the mean direction of
the radially-outward-going π⁻ (13 of 32; inward-going ones would have to
cross the ring and are written off; 6 project into the bore). In the demo
statistics 1 of the 6 captured π⁻ survives to decay in the channel,
i.e. ~10⁻³ μ⁻ per injected proton — the rest hit the bore wall within the
first turn (transverse momentum too large). Capture, not production, is
the design bottleneck — as in every π → μ front end — and this interface
(`make_channel_input.py --window/--bore`, channel `BSOL/RCH/LCH`) is
where to iterate.

## Optimized configuration: 3 GeV / beryllium / D-T stopping target

`scripts/run_3gev_chain.sh` runs the whole optimized chain. The goal here
is **mu- delivered and range-stopped (Bragg peak) in a liquid D-T target**
downstream of the capture solenoid + decay channel.

Optimization choices, each backed by a scan in this repo:

* **3 GeV protons** (energy scan): pi- per interaction grows 0.061 -> 0.31
  from 1 -> 3 GeV while the per-GeV cost flattens; beyond ~5 GeV the ring
  grows fast and the cascade model (BERT) thins.  The *same k = 3.6
  lattice* is reused, scaled to R0 = 20.30 m (Brho = 12.76 T m) - scaling
  FFAs are scale-invariant, and the tracked tunes reproduce exactly
  (Qx = 2.342, Qy = 1.220; closed orbit 20119.4 mm vs 20118.6 predicted).
* **10 mm beryllium target** (material scan at 3 GeV, C/Be/W at equal
  lambda-fraction): Be wins every axis that matters for a *recirculating*
  internal target - escaping pi-(+mu-) per interaction 0.361 (C 0.354,
  W 0.299), pi-/pi+ 0.83, RF energy per interaction lambda*dE/dx = 124 MeV
  (C 154, W 220), and scattering lambda/X0 = 1.2 (C 2.0, W 28: tungsten
  destroys recirculation).
* **RF**: ~401 MHz (h = 173), V_eff = 3.8 MV at phi_s ~ -30 deg - the ring
  is *above* transition at 3 GeV (gamma = 4.2 > gamma_t = 2.14) so the
  stable phase sits on the falling side; calibrated V_eff = 0.217 MeV per
  MV/m (transit-time prediction 0.223).
* **Capture**: the pi- fan out along the wall but originate in the 10 mm
  target spot, so the 0.6 m-bore, 5 T channel mouth is placed at the
  waist of the fan (scanned in `make_channel_input.py`): 42 % of all
  escaping pi-/mu- enter the channel.
* **40 m decay channel** then the **D-T target** (liquid D-T, rho = 0.21,
  0.6 m radius, 3 m long): a degrader scan showed *no degrader* is
  optimal - the soft half of the mu- spectrum supplies the stops and
  graphite only wastes muons.  21 of 42 surviving mu- range out inside
  the D-T volume (median Bragg depth 81 cm; half stop within the first
  ~0.8 m).

**Chain budget (800 protons, QGSP_BERT):**

| stage | per proton | energy cost |
|---|---|---|
| pi- + mu- escaping the ring | 0.270 | 11.5 GeV / pi- |
| captured into the channel (42 %) | 0.113 | 27 GeV |
| mu- at channel end (40 m) | 0.044 | 71 GeV |
| **mu- stopped in the D-T target** | **0.026** | **118 GeV / stopped mu-** |

(Invested energy = 3 GeV beam + ~0.10 GeV RF per proton.  Statistical
errors ~20 %.)  For context: the muCF literature assumes ~5 GeV per muon
*delivered*; this chain reaches 11.5 GeV per pi- *produced* - protons on
Be at 3 GeV vs the literature's deuterons on W at 3.6 GeV explains most
of the remaining production gap - and the pi- -> stopped-mu- conversion
(10 %) is now the dominant loss.  The next levers are capture (bore,
field, solid angle), channel length vs decay, and a momentum-graded
absorber or bent-solenoid momentum selection in front of the D-T cell.

Figures: `plots/3gev_ring_layout.png`, `plots/3gev_energy_recovery.png`,
`plots/3gev_pion_yield.png`, `plots/stopping_dt.png` (Bragg stop profile
and chain budget), and the updated `plots/energy_scan.png` with both ring
working points.

New pieces: `pion_decay_channel.g4bl` grew the STOP stage (degrader +
D-T cell + `beamlossntuple` end-point recording); `scripts/analyze_stops.py`
counts Bragg stops; `make_channel_input.py` places the channel mouth at
the acceptance waist and zeroes the ring-exit timestamps (the channel has
no RF, and stale times would trip its maxTime cut); `make_plots.py
--preset 3gev` regenerates the figures for the scaled ring.

## Knobs to play with

* `TGT` (mm): target thickness per pass. Thicker = fewer turns and more RF
  voltage per turn; V_eff·cos φ_s must equal ΔE_turn, with V_eff =
  0.2025 MeV per MV/m of `GRAD` — rerun `scripts/tune_rf.py` after changing
  the target.
* `KE0`: the same fixed-field ring accepts other injection energies; the
  orbit moves radially as r ∝ p^(1/(k+1)) (≈ +246 mm at 1.2 GeV, the outer
  chamber limits at ≈ 1.3 GeV). Get matching `RCO/RSTR/TREV` from
  `scripts/find_closed_orbit.py --p0 <MeV/c>`.
* lattice: `--k`, `--bendF`, `--thF`, `--fringe` ... in the map generator;
  after regenerating, redo the closed-orbit finder and the RF calibration.
  (k sets the tunes: k = 4.3 gave Qx = 2.54/Qy = 0.88, too close to
  resonances; k = 3.6 gives 2.34/1.22.)
* target material: swap `GRAPHITE` for `Be`, `B4C`, ... in the decks.
* capture: `--window`, `--bore` in `make_channel_input.py`; `BSOL`, `RCH`,
  `LCH` in the channel deck.

## Idealizations (read before believing a number)

* No injection hardware: protons start on the closed orbit. (A real
  machine would use H⁻ stripping injection through the same foil/target,
  as in KURRI's ERIT.)
* The RF cavity is a pure field (zero-thickness walls/windows). The
  ~11 MV/m CW gradient implied by a 5 mm target is demanding; a real
  design would split it over several cells and/or use a thinner target
  (1 mm ⇒ ~0.4 MeV/turn ⇒ ~0.5 MV total).
* The cell field map covers |y| ≤ 120 mm with a 1st+3rd order Maxwell
  expansion off the midplane — fine for the ≲ 40 mm excursions seen here.
* The capture channel is a geometric acceptance filter plus a uniform
  solenoid; no field matching between ring fringe and channel.
* π production near 1 GeV is model-dependent: compare physics lists
  (`physics QGSP_BIC`, `INCLXX` variants) and thin-target data (HARP)
  before quoting absolute yields.

## How this setup was validated

1. `probe_field.g4bl` + `fieldntuple`: the assembled ring field matches the
   analytic design (B(r₀) exact, (r/r₀)^k scaling, 12-fold symmetry to
   machine precision, F/D azimuthal profile as designed).
2. `ring_test.g4bl`: single 1 GeV proton stays bounded for > 4500 turns;
   closed orbit located to < 1 mm; tunes from turn-by-turn FFT.
3. `scripts/tune_rf.py`: measured V_eff(GRAD) agrees with the analytic
   transit-time factor of the 405 MHz pillbox to a few %; phase offset of
   the timing bookkeeping is 0.7°.
4. Full ring: mean beam energy flat at 1000 MeV over ≥ 80 turns with
   target+RF, exponential survival with the expected interaction rate, and
   the escaping π⁻/π⁺ ratio consistent with the standalone target study.
5. Fixed-field check: injecting 1.2 GeV into the *same* field finds a
   stable orbit at r = 9166.5 mm vs. 9166.3 mm predicted by the scaling
   law r ∝ p^(1/(k+1)) — sub-mm agreement.
